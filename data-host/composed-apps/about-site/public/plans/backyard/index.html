Design basis

Sheet 01 of 09

What was decided

  • The deck sits at the slider. It runs 25 ft from the west end of the chimney to 18 in short of the house's east corner, and 20 ft out from the wall. The sliding door sits in an angled bay, and the deck is notched around the bay's three faces and the chimney. The two outer corners are cut at 45 degrees, 3.5 ft each way, so there are no square corners to walk into. Dining by the slider; the deck's west end is left open. A vented composite skirt closes the underside. The slider is its only door.
  • Cooking on the east side. The Blackstone griddle (62.5 x 22 in) and the smoker (49 x 27 in, a pellet smoker assumed) stand side by side with their backs to the east rail, each 3 ft from the rail and the house, with a 3 ft aisle to the table.
  • Freestanding framing. The house is brick veneer, and a deck ledger may not be fastened to veneer, so the deck stands on two three-ply beams and 4 posts each. The surface sits 1 in below the door thresholds.
  • One stair. A 5 ft stair off the west side (4 risers) goes down onto the patio. The walkway around the deck is reached from the patio.
  • A 16 x 16 ft stone patio off the deck's west side, dry-laid buff flagstone.
  • The fire ring is centred on the patio's northwest corner. A 14 ft round stone apron shares that centre, so the patio reads as a square with a circle bulging out of its corner, and a 52 in stone ring with a 36 in steel liner sits at the centre.
  • A stone seat wall 18 in tall and 14 in deep, capped, curves around the north half of the fire ring and runs east along the patio's north edge, stopping 3 ft short of the deck so the walkway still meets the patio. It holds back the fill on the low side and seats the north half of the ring; three chairs take the south half.
  • The outdoor sofa set moves to the patio, on its southwest part: sofa on the west edge facing east, coffee table, two chairs opposite, with a clear path from the stair to the fire.
  • A stone raised herb bed 18 in tall between the patio and the house, from the AC screen to the chimney. It has its own back wall 4 in off the brick, so no soil sits against the veneer or its weep holes.
  • A screen around the AC: vinyl privacy lattice over outdoor mass-loaded vinyl on the north side and an east gate, the sides facing the patio and the fire, 2 ft off the unit. The west side toward the fence and the top stay open so the condenser draws fresh air and its hot exhaust does not circle back in. No painting, no upkeep.
  • A stone walkway of the same flagstone, 3 ft wide, around the deck's north and east sides, following the cut corners and joining the patio at the northwest.
  • Shade by a cantilever umbrella over the table, bolted to a steel plate over blocking in the framing.
  • Five American Pillar arborvitae in one row 4 ft off the north fence, centred on the slider. They grow 20 to 30 ft tall and stay 3 to 5 ft wide, and they take wet clay.
  • A French drain along the east fence into the existing drain in the northeast corner.
  • Contracted, not built by the owner. Demo, deck, patio, fire ring, drain and planting go to contractors. The owner plants the herb bed, hangs the AC screen, and buys the umbrella plate and the ring liner.

Assumptions, revision A

  • The back of the house faces north. The garage is at the east end of the front; the side walk runs down the east side to the back.
  • Lot about 70 ft wide; house about 52 ft wide; back wall to north fence about 50 ft. All read from drone photos and marked est.
  • Grade falls 30 in from the house to the north fence and about 6 in from west to east, lowest at the existing drain in the northeast corner.
  • Door thresholds 25 in above the ground at the back wall. The deck stands 27 to 41 in above grade, so it gets guards on every open side.
  • Heavy clay soil that drains slowly, a 42 in frost depth for footings, growing zone 6.
  • The existing northeast drain can take the French drain; its pipe size and depth are not known yet.

Measure for revision B

  • Back wall to north fence, and each side of the house to its fence.
  • Threshold height at the slider.
  • Chimney, bay, slider and window positions along the back wall, and the AC's footprint.
  • Fall from the house to the north fence and to the drain (string line and level).
  • Pipe size and depth at the northeast drain.
Fire ring distances

The ring stands 16.8 ft from the house wall, 13.8 ft from the deck's west edge and 12.8 ft from the foot of the west stair. The owner set its position. For reference, the fire code the township adopts lists 25 ft from structures for an open wood fire and 15 ft for a listed portable fire pit; the permit conversation starts from there. Keep a spark screen on the ring and a hose on the patio side.

Deck contractor

Sheet 03 of 09

A freestanding pressure-treated frame with composite boards, composite guards with black aluminium balusters, and one stair. 461 sq ft of deck surface. The contractor pulls the permit and draws the framing to the code tables; the numbers here are the design intent for the bid and what the model checks.

Scope for the deck bid

  • Demolish and haul away the existing deck, lattice skirting, rock bed, paver fire ring and edging.
  • Permit, drawings, footing, framing and final inspections.
  • 8 posts on 12 in piers 42 in deep plus stair footings; 6x6 ground-contact posts; three-ply 2x10 beams; 2x10 joists at 12 in on centre with hangers and ties; joist tape.
  • Composite decking about 1155 lin ft (73 16 ft boards equivalent), a light tan or grey, hidden fasteners, a breaker board at the splice, matching fascia.
  • 36 in composite guard with black aluminium balusters, about 53 lin ft (9 6 ft sections) including the two cut corners, handrails on the stair.
  • Blocking and one owner-supplied umbrella plate, bolted through.
  • Skirt: horizontal composite boards matching the fascia on every open side, 3/8 in gaps for air (about 112 sq ft, 251 lin ft of board), a 3 ft hinged access panel on the east side.

Things to ask each bidder

  • Piers or helical piles, and why. Helical piles avoid eight holes in wet clay and are inspected by torque.
  • Board brand and line, colour, and the warranty on boards and fasteners.
  • How they hold the surface 1 in under the slider threshold.
  • Who calls MISS DIG and who pulls the permit.
  • A price per added foot if revision B changes the size.

Patio, fire ring, herbs and AC screen contractor owner

Sheet 04 of 09

A 16 ft square of dry-laid buff flagstone off the deck's west side, and a 14 ft round apron centred on its northwest corner with the 52 in stone fire ring at the centre: about 357 sq ft together. The same stone, 3 ft wide, runs around the deck's north and east sides and its cut corners: about 141 sq ft more. 498 sq ft in all.

Scope for the stone bid

  • Excavate, compact, fabric, 9.2 cu yd of 21AA, 1.5 cu yd of stone dust, about 6.8 tons of flagstone at 80 sq ft per ton plus 10 percent.
  • Seat wall: about 27.3 lin ft, 158 wall stones in two wythes and a flat cap, around the apron's north half and along the patio's north edge.
  • Fire ring: about 47 wall stones in four courses and a flat cap, around the owner-supplied 36 in liner.
  • Deeper edge stones on the patio's low north and west sides.
  • Raised herb bed: about 118 wall blocks on a compacted base, back wall 4 in off the brick, flat cap.
  • Joints stone dust or polymeric sand, priced both ways.

Stone

  • Chosen: buff Wisconsin limestone flagstone (Chilton or Fond du Lac) or Tennessee tan sandstone, 1.5 to 2 in thick, dry-laid. Light cream to tan, next to the tan brick.
  • Fallback: a buff concrete paver field with real stone kept for the ring, its cap and the seat wall cap.
  • Pick stone in person, one pallet run for the whole job. Match the ring's wall stone to the flagstone.

Evergreens and drain contractor

Sheet 05 of 09

Five American Pillar arborvitae planted 5 to 6 ft tall in one row, and a French drain along the east fence. The drain goes in first, while the ground is open.

Planting scope

  • Five American Pillar (Thuja occidentalis 'American Pillar'), 5 to 6 ft, from one nursery with its one-year warranty.
  • Holes twice the root ball wide and no deeper; the root flare 1 to 2 in above grade.
  • 3 in of shredded mulch in a 3 ft ring, kept off the trunks.

Drain scope

  • About 66 lin ft of trench, 8 sticks of 4 in perforated SDR35, 4.9 cu yd of washed stone, about 466 sq ft of fabric.
  • Tie into the existing northeast drain with a proper fitting or a cored and grouted entry.
  • Rear-yard drains often sit in a drainage easement: the bidder confirms with the township that a private tie-in is allowed.
  • Test with a hose at the upstream cleanout before backfilling.

Care guide

Sheet 09 of 09

Fire ring

  • Dry hardwood only; a spark screen on the ring whenever it's lit; a hose on the patio side.
  • Keep the fire small: the deck's west stair is 12.8 ft away and composite will scorch.
  • Ashes cold for 24 hours before they go anywhere. Scoop the bottom gravel clean each spring.

Evergreens

  • Water deeply once a week the first two summers. American Pillar holds its narrow shape without shearing; trim only stray leaders.

Deck and stone

  • Wash composite with soap and a soft brush twice a year. The Blackstone and the smoker stand on mats, 3 ft from the house and the rail; composite will scorch and grease stains.
  • Umbrellas closed and tied in wind.
  • Top up patio and walkway joints with stone dust each spring.

Herbs and drain

  • Top the herb bed with 1 in of compost each spring; perennials (thyme, oregano, chives) at the ends, annuals in the middle.
  • Rake leaves off the drain strip each fall; run a hose into the cleanout once a year.
  • AC screen: nothing to paint or seal. Pull leaves out from behind it in the fall so the condenser breathes.
