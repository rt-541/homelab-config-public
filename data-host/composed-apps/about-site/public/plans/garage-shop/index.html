Garage Shop

A one-car woodshop in an 18 by 20 ft attached garage with a 12 ft ceiling and a 6 in concrete landing across the back. The truck keeps the center bay with its nose over the landing, the potting station and the machine bay take the west wall with the bulk rack on the landing behind them, and the shop takes the east wall: a locking wall-hung tool cabinet, a rolling flip bench with the table saw sunk flush at one end, the DCS785 miter saw on a flip section in the middle and the DW735 planer on a flip section at the other end, a mobile dust cart pairing the 16 gal Stealthsonic vac with a cyclone, the compressor beside it, and a 100 A subpanel fed from the basement with dedicated circuits for every stationary tool. Everything that cuts is on wheels and parks against the wall.

Design basis

What was decided

  • Rolling shop, fixed walls. The flip bench and the dust cart roll out into the aisle or the empty bay to work and park against the east wall, the bench under the cabinet. The only fixed pieces are on the walls, so the bay stays a clear 7 by 17 ft rectangle for the Maverick or the Forester.
  • Garden west, shop east, bulk storage at the back. The potting station takes the front-left corner, the machine bay and the hook rail the rest of the west wall; the bulk rack stands on the landing in the back-left corner with the bags under it, and the landing east of the truck's nose stays empty as the way out of the house door. The tool cabinet, the flip bench under it, the dust cart, the compressor and the subpanel take the east wall.
  • The landing is the wheel stop. The truck parks with its front tires 6 in short of the curb and its nose 13 in over the landing (the bumper clears the 6 in step), 11 in short of the house door's step. Nothing lives on the landing in front of the truck, and nothing on it east of the nose.
  • Table saw in the bench. The DWE7491 (to be bought) sits in the bench's end bay on a platform that puts its table flush with the bench top; the blade runs along the bench so the flat sections are its outfeed. Its 20 A receptacle is C3.
  • Wall-hung locking cabinet. 48 W x 40 H x 12 D case with two 4 in deep door boxes on continuous hinges, bottom edge 48 in off the floor. Hardened hasps with discus padlocks, hinge screws hidden inside the boxes, and the French cleat pinned from inside so the case cannot be lifted off while locked.
  • One flip bench for the three machines. 92 x 30 in on six casters: table saw at one end, the 56 lb DCS785 on a 4 in torsion-box flip section in the middle, the 92 lb DW735 on another at the far end. Each flip section carries its tool on one face and a 4.5 in riser bench top on the other, so with both tools down it is one flat bench at 37.75 in, with the miter up its table is at bench height with a wing either side, and with the planer up the bench is its infeed. Spring index plungers and draw latches lock each section at 0 and 180; concrete cast into the risers puts each section's centre of gravity on its shaft. Neither tool is ever lifted again.
  • Dust cart. A 38 x 24 in plywood base on 3 in casters with the DeWalt 16 gal Stealthsonic drum braced in a 2x4 corral and, beside it, a 12 in pedestal carrying a Dust Deputy cyclone on its 5 gal bucket, with an 8 in spine for the strap and the handle. The two tippiest things in a shop become one 85 lb cart with a 32 degree tip angle, and the vac's head stays free to lift off.
  • Printed where a print beats a purchase. A Prusa Core One is on hand, so hose cuffs, the planer reducer, clips, grommets, battery docks and bit holders are printed in PETG from the model's STEP files instead of bought; each sheet lists its printed parts and the shopping list leaves them out.
  • Nothing heavy on the floor. Bags sit on the potting station's 6 in shelf or stacked on the landing under the bulk rack; totes start at 30 in above the floor; the pot well puts a pot's rim at counter height so filling is a scoop, not a lift; the EGO mower and snowblower roll in and out of a flat bay.
  • 100 A subpanel, permit-grade. Fed from the basement main through the house wall at the back-right corner to the east wall, with 20 A circuits for the table saw, the flip bench's drop, and the compressor and vac, four LED work lights (two over the work zone, two over the plant side), a 50 A EV circuit roughed in for a future charger, a 30 A heater circuit, GFCI general receptacles and a 15 A lighting circuit.

Why one flip bench and not three stands

Three stands take three footprints and give the miter saw and the table saw no outfeed. One bench mounts all three permanently in 92 x 30 in, and the two flip sections turn its flat top into the miter station's wings and the table saw's outfeed the moment a tool goes down. Each flip is ten seconds: pull the plunger, turn, it snaps home, close the latch. The price is two 1 in shafts, four $9 bearings, two plungers, two latches, 210 lb of concrete and a careful hour aligning the bushings.

Why a subpanel and not four circuits fished from the basement

Four new 20 A circuits plus a 50 A EV circuit plus a heater circuit is six runs through the house. One 100 A feeder is one run, and every future circuit starts in the garage. The feeder is also the only part of this job that is expensive to change later, so it is sized for everything on the list running at once, including the EV charger that is not bought yet, set to 32 A.

Tool cabinet wall-hung, locking

Sheet 03 of 11

3/4 in birch plywood case, 48 W x 40 H x 12 D, on a French cleat with the bottom edge 48 in above the floor. Two 24 x 40 x 4 in door boxes on 40 in continuous hinges. Closed depth 17.5 in from the wall, counting the 1.5 in cleat stack behind the back.

Case

  • Sides 12 x 40, top and bottom 12 x 46.5 between the sides, pocket-screwed from the outside faces of top and bottom into the sides (plugs hidden by the doors).
  • Back 3/4 in, 48 x 40, overlaid on the case and screwed every 6 in. The back is structural: it carries the cleat load and the anti-lift screws.
  • Two fixed shelves 11.25 x 46.5 at 12 and 24 in above the case bottom, pocket-screwed. Shelf 2 sits 3/4 in back from the front edge to clear the door-box contents.
  • Top zone: the LBT rack screws to the back panel; a 2.5 in cleat strip on each side of it takes whatever does not fit the doors.
  • Case cleat 4 x 46.5, wall cleat 4 x 44, both 3/4 in ripped at 45 degrees. A 4 x 46.5 spacer at the bottom of the back keeps the case plumb.
  • Finish: two coats of water-based poly inside and out before assembly; the doors get it on both faces so they do not cup.

Doors and lock

  • Each door is a box: 3/4 face 24 x 40, 3/4 rails 3.25 in wide glued and brad-nailed around the face, so the box is 4 in deep outside. 1/2 in cleat strips inside for the hand tools; nothing over 5 lb per door.
  • Hinges: Everbilt 1-1/2 x 48 in continuous hinge cut to 40 in, one per door, screwed to the case side and the door rail with #6 x 5/8 screws. The screw heads are inside the closed box, unreachable when locked.
  • Meeting edge: the right door's face is 3/4 in wider than its box and overlaps the left door, so there is no gap for a pry bar. Close left, then right.
  • Locks: two Master Lock 730DPF hardened hasps across the meeting joint, 6 in from the top and bottom, with 40DPF discus padlocks (shrouded shackle, no bolt-cutter bite). The hasp's hinged leaf covers its own screws when closed.
  • Anti-lift: after hanging, drive two GRK #10 x 3-1/8 screws from inside the case through the back and both cleats at the stud positions. The case then cannot be lifted off the cleat without opening the doors.
  • Wall cleat: three studs, two GRK #10 x 3-1/8 per stud. Loaded cabinet about 250 lb; six structural screws in shear carry many times that.

Flip bench DWE7491 / DCS785 / DW735

Sheet 04 of 11

One rolling bench, 92 x 30 in, three bays between four 1.5 in panels: the table saw sits in the end bay with its table sunk flush to the bench top, the miter saw is on a flip section in the middle, the planer on a flip section at the other end. Each flip section is a 4 in torsion box on its own 1 in shaft with the tool on one face and a 4.5 in riser bench top on the other, so with both tools down the whole bench is flat at 37.75 in; with the miter up its table is at that same height and the saw section and the planer section are its wings; with the planer up the bench is its infeed. The blade runs along the bench, so the flat sections are 60 in of outfeed for the table saw. Detail page: /projects/garage-shop/flip-bench.

Sections

  • Bays, from the front: 24 in saw, 32 in miter, 30 in planer, between panels S, A, B and C of 1.5 in laminated plywood, 30 x 29, standing on a 2x4 base frame (two 92 in side rails on flat, a cross rail under every panel), six 3 in total-lock casters, a 3/4 x 8 in apron along the back. About 720 lb loaded.
  • Saw bay: the DWE7491 (55 lb, table 13 in above its feet, 22.7 x 29.2 footprint) stands on a platform at 24 in so its table is flush with the 3/4 top around it; the blade runs along the bench and the rip fence rails run across it, extending out the front for wide rips. Two shelves below the platform for blades, push sticks and the miter clamps.
  • Flip sections: a 27 in (miter) and a 25 in (planer) torsion box, 30 wide, 4 deep, 3/4 skins, end plates and two ribs, open along both long sides, carried on a 1 in shaft by a clamp-on flanged collar at each end plate; UCFL205-16 flange bearings on the panel faces inside each bay, 2.5 in of bay each end for the bearing and hub. The tool is bolted to a 3/4 plate that fastens to one skin with six 1/4-20 bolts into T-nuts; the other skin carries the riser.
  • Risers: a 4.5 in tall plywood box (3/4 top, 3/4 sides) on the free face of each flip section. In bench mode it is the bench top; with the tool up it hangs underneath. Its height is the DCS785's table height, which is what makes the wings flush with the miter table.
  • Heights: bench, miter table and saw table 37.75 in, planer bed 39.25 in. The shaft cannot go lower than 30.5 with 3 in casters because the hanging miter's corner has to clear the side rails; 2 in casters bring everything down 1 in.

Lock, balance, use

  • Lock per section: a 1/2 in spring index plunger with a pull knob through a steel bushing in the outer panel (A for the miter, C for the planer), 10 in off the shaft line toward the room, into one of two bushings in the box end plate so it indexes at 0 and 180; an adjustable draw latch on panel B pulls the box tight to it. Plunger home and latch closed before a tool runs.
  • Ballast is concrete cast into the riser: 1 in under the miter riser's top (63 lb) balances the 56 lb saw to 4 lb at the bench edge; 2.5 in under the planer riser's top (146 lb) brings the 92 lb planer to 14 lb, on the tool side of neutral so a section never runs away. Pour with the riser as its own form, top skin on after a week. Steel plate does the same job at four times the price.
  • Miter station: miter up, planer down, table saw as is: 24 in of wing on one side, 30 on the other, both at table height; the fence and stop go on the wings. Planing: planer up, miter down, boards feed along the bench over the flat miter section and out past the end. Ripping: both flip sections down, feed along the bench, 60 in of flush outfeed.
  • Parks along the east wall under the tool cabinet in bench mode, top at 37.75 in against the cabinet's 48 in bottom, between the compressor and the walkway. Roll it out before flipping a tool up.
  • Printed on the Core One: plunger keepers, a holster for the latch keepers, T-nut alignment jigs, a 2-1/2 in dust hood adapter for the DCS785 and a zero-clearance insert blank for the DWE7491.

Dust cart DXV16P + Dust Deputy

Two bays side by side on one doubled plywood base: the DeWalt 16 gal Stealthsonic drum stands inside a 2x4 corral with its own caster base removed, and beside it a 12 in pedestal carries the Dust Deputy bucket in a U cradle with the cyclone on its lid. An 8 in spine on the outer end takes the bucket strap and the handle. Long and low, nothing stacked over the vac: its head lifts off in place for the filter, and the cyclone drains forward without unhooking a hose. Measure the drum with its caster base off (the base is a separate molded dolly the drum sits in) and the bucket's bottom before cutting; the corral's inside width and the cradle hole are the two numbers, the model re-runs the fit, rail-height, hose-fall and tip checks from them.

Build

  • Base: two 38 x 24 pieces glued and screwed into one 1.5 in slab. Casters at the outside corners, plates flush with the edges, two braked; 85 lb loaded, so four casters rated 75 lb or more each.
  • Pedestal: two 14 x 12 sides, a 22.5 x 12 wall between the pedestal and the corral, and the 14 x 24 cradle on top with a 12 in hole cut open to the bay side as a U. The spine is a 14 x 40 piece on the outer end, full width for the bottom 12 in and 8 in wide above, with the handle slot 3.5 in from the top and two strap slots at the bucket's half height.
  • Corral: four 2x4 posts 24 in tall screwed down through the base at the bay's corners, a 2x4 ring on flat between them at the base and a second ring on edge with its top at 16 in, 3 in construction screws through the posts. Inside the rails the drum has 3/4 in each side; the rail sits 6 in above the drum's centre of gravity, so the drum cannot tip and no strap is needed. The rings are the handholds when the cart is lifted over a threshold.
  • Cradle open to the bay: release the bucket strap, unclip the lid, lift cyclone and lid an inch and slide the bucket sideways out of the U. The drum never moves to empty the cyclone; the head lifts off in place to clean the filter.
  • Glue plus 1-5/8 to 2 in screws throughout; predrill every screw that goes into a plywood edge.

Hoses, power, stability

  • Everything is 2-1/2 in: the DXV16P ships with a 7 ft x 2-1/2 hose and the Dust Deputy 2.5 takes 2-1/2 cuffs, so no bought reducer. The kit's short hose goes cyclone outlet to drum inlet, the vac's 7 ft hose goes cyclone inlet to the tool. The DW735 uses the printed 4 to 2-1/2 reducer and fills the 5 gal bucket in a few boards; plane into the chip bag for batches and use the cart for the miter saw, sander, router and cleanup.
  • The vac's switch stays on its head, in reach. A remote-control outlet or an auto-start tool switch on C5 turns it on with the tool. 20 ft cord reaches C5 from the parking spot and the C4 drop from the work zone.
  • Stability: 85 lb with the center of gravity 16 in up on a 20 in track across the narrow way tips at 32 degrees, or 19 lb of sideways shove at the handle; the long way is far stiffer. Push from the handle slot, never the cyclone.
  • Parks end-on in the east wall's front corner beside the compressor, 24 in along the wall and 39 in out, 4 in inside the door track; the aisle stays 49 in with the truck in.
  • Printed on the Core One (PETG): two 2-1/2 in hose cuffs with a 1/4 turn lock so the cyclone hoses come off without a clamp, the 4 to 2-1/2 in reducer for the DW735's port, a snap hose clip and a cord clip on the pedestal wall, grommets for the strap and handle slots so the strap does not saw the plywood, and a cap for the cyclone inlet when the cart is parked. The STEP of the cart is the reference for fitting them.

  4. Mount the subpanel on the east wall 3 ft from the back corner, just past the side door's frame, top breaker center at 72 in, its working space on the flat floor in front.   5. Order of operations that matters

Electrical before anything is fixed to a wall; the cabinet's receptacle is behind the case. Operating guide

Sheet 11 of 11

Daily

  • Roll the bench and the dust cart into the work zone, lock all casters, plug the bench into the C4 drop (table saw on C3) and the vac into C5 or C6, never the same circuit as the planer. Nothing goes under the flip sections' swing; the frame is open there on purpose. The bench only flips a tool up once it is out from under the cabinet.
  • Flip sections: plunger home and latch closed before starting a tool. Miter head locked down and slide locked before every flip; open the latch, pull the plunger, turn, listen for the plunger to snap, close the latch. Miter station is miter up, planer down; planing is planer up, miter down; ripping is both down.
  • Planer chips: the DW735 blows into its chip bag; the dust cart takes the miter saw, sander, router and cleanup. Empty the bucket at two-thirds, sideways out of the U, without lifting the cyclone off; the 16 gal drum only sees what the cyclone misses.
  • End of session: tools back on their shelves, doors closed left then right, both padlocks on, both carts parked against the east wall spine and shaft to the wall, casters locked, the truck's front tires to the curb.

Seasonal

  • April: snowblower out of the bay onto the dolly, mower in. October: reverse. Batteries stored at 40 to 60 percent indoors in winter.
  • Ice-melt bags onto the landing under the bulk rack in November; potting mix onto the potting station's shelf in March, the open bag nearest the bin.
  • Heater: thermostat at 45 F when idle, 60 F to work. A 5 kW unit heater lifts this 18 x 20 ft, 12 ft tall garage (4,300 cu ft, half again the volume of an 8 ft room) about 20 F above outdoor temperature at steady state; on a 10 F day expect 30 to 35 F unless the door seals are good, and the warm air pools under the 12 ft ceiling, so a ceiling fan on low pays for itself. Never store anything within 3 ft of its face.

Electrical

  • Test every GFCI monthly with its button. A tripped C6 kills the west and east wall general receptacles only; the bench, the table saw and the compressor are on their own circuits.
  • When the EV arrives: hardwire the EVSE to the C1 box, set it to 32 A (or 40 A if the charger allows) and schedule it after 9 pm. Charging and planing at the same time is fine; charging, planing, dust collecting and heating at once is the one case the feeder is sized for but not designed to live at.
  • The subpanel main is the garage kill switch. Label every breaker with the C-number from sheet 07.

Maintenance

  • Bearings: a shot of grease in each of the four zerks twice a year; check the set screws, the flanged collars' bolts and the latch tension when you do. Re-check each section's balance whenever a tool or a plate changes.
  • Padlocks: a drop of dry lube in the keyway each fall; discus locks freeze if water sits in them.
  • Cleat screws: once a year, open the cabinet and check the two anti-lift screws are snug.
  • Bulk rack: nothing above 60 in heavier than a light bin; load it standing on the landing. Potting station: empty the well platform's tray and wipe the bin lip after wet work; re-oil the top each spring.
  • Dust cart: strap snug and the casters' brakes on when parked; check the hose cuffs seat every few sessions, a leak at the cyclone inlet drops its separation to nothing.
