Basement Spool Cabinet

A sealed plywood cabinet one IKEA BILLY wide, with two OXBERG tempered-glass doors, that rests at 10 to 20 percent humidity on desiccant alone. 56 spools on edge in cradle rails, seven per row at 4 in pitch, two rows deep, plus cubby overflow. Pocket-screwed from three sheets of 3/4 plywood; drying is done in a bought box dehydrator, not in the cabinet.

Design basis

Sheet 01 of 06

What was decided

  • Two jobs, two boxes. The cabinet is airtight and passive and you build it. Drying happens in a box food dehydrator you buy used: it reaches 70 C with fan airflow and a timer, which is everything the heater box on the earlier revisions did, for $25 to $100.
  • One plywood case, one bay. 31.5 in wide, 76 in tall on a 3.5 in plinth, 18.75 in deep: the width of an IKEA BILLY, so it takes a pair of OXBERG glass doors with their own hinges. A fixed 1x3 center stile at the front carries the seal where the doors meet.
  • Spools stand on edge in cradle rails, two rows deep, seven per row at a 4 in pitch, back row on a 2.5 in riser so its rim stands 1.75 in above the front row and you lift it out by the rim. 56 on rails; the cubby at the top takes up to 18 more laid flat.
  • Shelves are fixed and pocket-screwed into the sides. No adjustable standards. At a 30 in span the 3/4 in shelf with its 2x3 risers needs no apron.
  • Seal: EPDM bulb weatherstrip on the case edges and the center stile, with two magnetic catches per door. A made-to-size magnetic refrigerator gasket is the upgrade if the flashlight test or the hygrometer says the bulb seal leaks.

Assumptions

  • 1 kg spool: 200 mm (8 in) diameter, up to 75 mm (3 in) wide. That width sets the 4 in pitch.
  • 2 kg spool: 200 mm diameter, up to 140 mm (5.5 in) wide. One takes two slots on the rails; the dehydrator takes one at a time.
  • Basement air: about 65 F. Summer 60 to 70 percent humidity, winter 30 to 40 percent.
  • Tools on hand: miter saw, circular saw, drill, brad nailer, pocket hole jig. No router or table saw assumed.
  • Shelf is PLA and PETG with some TPU. Nothing that needs more than 70 C to dry.
Why the storage cabinet needs no heater

Heat does not remove water, it only changes the humidity reading. Summer basement air carries about 10 g of water per cubic meter. Warmed to 35 C it reads 25 percent, at 40 C it reads 20 percent, at 45 C it reads 15 percent, and you pay 150 to 250 W around the clock for that. A sealed cabinet with 10 lb of silica gel sits at 10 to 15 percent at basement temperature for free. Every door opening lets in roughly 3 g of water, so the gel needs an oven recharge every few weeks in summer and every month or two in winter.

Why a dehydrator and not a heater box

The heater box on revisions A to C cost about $270 in parts and a day of wiring to reach 65 C with a fan and a thermostat. A box food dehydrator is that same thing with a warranty: 35 to 74 C in 1 degree steps, rear fan, 48 hour timer, and it exhausts the moist air instead of trapping it. With the trays out a six-tray unit holds three 1 kg spools on edge or one 2 kg; a ten-tray unit holds four or two. Used ones run $25 to $100. So the dryer is a purchase, and this drawing set is only the cabinet.

Carcass

  • 3/4 in plywood throughout: sides 18 x 76, top and bottom 30 x 18 between the sides, four shelves and one cubby shelf 30 x 17, back 31.5 x 76 overlaying sides, top and bottom, glued and screwed every 6 in.
  • Pocket screws throughout: Kreg jig at the 3/4 in setting, 1-1/4 in coarse screws, glue in every joint. Top and bottom into the sides with pockets on their hidden faces; each shelf into the sides with three pockets per end on its underside. Run a bead of clear silicone along every interior seam after assembly and over any pocket that faces inward.
  • Fixed shelves, bottoms at 6, 20, 34 and 48 in above the case floor; the cubby shelf at 62 in. Fixed shelves tie the sides together, so the case is rigid before the back goes on.
  • Two coats of dewaxed shellac on every interior face, applied flat before assembly since fixed shelves make the inside corners hard to reach. Bare plywood soaks up and releases water for weeks; sealed plywood does not. Cure 3 days with doors open before loading gel.
  • Center stile: 1x3 poplar, 74.5 long, glued and pocket-screwed between top and bottom at the front, flush with the case edges. It gives the two doors 1.25 in each to close against and carries their seal.
  • Plinth: 2x4 frame 27.5 x 15, pocket-screwed with 2-1/2 in coarse screws, set back 2 in at the front. Two L-brackets from the top rear into studs. Loaded weight is about 250 lb with 56 spools and it is 6 ft 8 in tall.

Doors and seal

  • Two OXBERG glass doors, 15.5 x 75.75 in, 4 mm tempered glass in a 3/4 in fiberboard frame, concealed hinges and knobs included, $50 each. Glass is as good a vapor barrier as acrylic and it does not creep, craze or need screws through it.
  • Overlay: the doors hinge on the sides and meet over the center stile with a 1/8 in gap, covering the 31.5 in case the way they cover a BILLY. Door height 75.75 against the 76 in case leaves 1/8 in top and bottom.
  • Hinges: the 35 mm cups are already bored in the doors. Hang each mounting plate on its hinge, hold the door closed in position with the reveals set, mark the two plate screw holes on the side, drill 5 mm, drive the included Euro screws. Adjust the hinge in and out until the door just kisses the seal all the way round.
  • Seal, default: Frost King V25BK 5/16 x 1/4 in EPDM D-section on the case front edges and both faces of the center stile, not on the door. The hollow bulb seals with almost no closing force. Two magnetic catches per door, 11 to 20 lb, at 12 in from the top and bottom on the stile side, plates on the back of the door. The OXBERG hinges are self-closing and the magnets hold the door onto the bulb.
  • Seal, upgrade: a made-to-size magnetic refrigerator gasket on each door's back perimeter ($45 a door) pulling onto 3/4 in perforated galvanized hanger strap glued along the case edges and the stile. That is a fridge door. Fit it only if the flashlight test shows light or the cabinet will not hold under 20 percent.
  • One 3/8 in hole at the bottom back corner for the fan's USB cable, filled with silicone after.

Desiccant and monitoring

  • One half-sheet aluminum pan, 13 x 18, holding 5 lb of orange indicating silica gel spread 1 in deep. Surface area matters more than depth. A second 5 lb bag on the cubby shelf halves the recharge interval if you open the doors a lot.
  • A 120 mm USB fan on the floor blowing across the pan, running always. Moving air makes the gel work several times faster than still air. Draw is under 2 W.
  • One hygrometer at mid height, readable through the glass. A Bluetooth one with phone alerts warns you when it climbs past 20 percent.
  • Recharge the gel when the beads turn green or the reading passes 20 percent: spread on the pan, 250 F in the oven for 2 to 3 hours until orange again. Expect every 3 to 5 weeks in summer, 2 months in winter.

Build sequence

Sheet 05 of 06

Case

  1. Break down sheets A to C to the cut list. Label every part and mark which face is inside.
  2. Everything flat on the bench. Drill the pocket holes: three per end on the underside of each shelf, along the hidden faces of top and bottom, and two at each end of the center stile. Glue and brad the 1x2 rails and glue and screw the 2x3 risers from below on all four spool shelves. Mark the shelf lines on both sides at 6, 20, 34, 48 and 62 in above the bottom panel.
  3. Two coats of shellac on every interior face while it is still flat: sides, top, bottom, back and the shelf assemblies. Let it dry overnight.
  4. Lay one side flat. Glue and pocket-screw the bottom and top to it, then the five shelves, square to the marks. Bring the second side down onto the assembly and pocket-screw it the same way. Check diagonals.
  5. Glue and pocket-screw the center stile in at the front, flush with the case edges. Glue and screw the back on, screws every 6 in. The back squares the box.
  6. Silicone every interior seam, including the shelf ends and the back, and any pocket that faces inward. Leave the box open to cure for 3 days.
  7. Build the plinth, set the case on it, screw down through the bottom. Stand it in place and anchor the top rear to a stud.

Doors and seal

  1. Apply the EPDM D-section to the case front edges and both faces of the center stile. Butt the corners; do not stretch it.
  2. Unbox the OXBERG doors and clip the hinge arms into their cups. Hang a mounting plate on each hinge.
  3. Hold the left door closed in position: 1/8 in reveal at top and bottom, its outer edge flush with the case side. Mark the plate holes through the plates, swing the door away, drill 5 mm, drive the Euro screws. Repeat for the right door.
  4. Adjust each hinge in and out until the door just compresses the bulb all the way round with no gap at the corners. Fit two magnetic catches per door on the stile side, plates on the door back.
  5. Flashlight test: close the doors, kill the lights, and shine a flashlight around the door edges from inside. Any light is a leak; adjust the hinge or add a second layer of D-section there.
  6. Gel pan and fan in, cable through the 3/8 in hole, silicone the hole, hygrometer at mid height. Close it. It should read under 20 percent within 48 hours and under 15 within a week as the plywood finishes giving up water. If it stalls above 20, that is when you order the fridge gaskets.

Dehydrator: trays out except the bottom one, spools on edge, a hygrometer inside on the first run to check the dial. Set the timer for the hours above; the spool is done when the hygrometer next to it reads under 10 percent.

Routine

  • New filament: a dehydrator cycle first, then into the cabinet. Never put a fresh spool from a shipping bag into the cabinet; its water goes straight into your gel.
  • Printing: pull from the cabinet and print. PLA and PETG that sit under 20 percent stay printable for months. Nylon needs the dehydrator on its top setting and a long cycle.
  • Cabinet reads over 20 percent: recharge the gel. If it climbs within a week of a recharge, check the bulb seal with a flashlight from inside and re-silicone the fan cable hole.
  • Dehydrator dial: a kitchen dial can read 5 C off. Trust the hygrometer inside it and the marks you made on the first run, not the printed numbers.
