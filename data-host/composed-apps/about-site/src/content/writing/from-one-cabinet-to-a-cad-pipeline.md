The first physical plan on this site was a [cabinet for 3D printer filament](/projects/spool-cabinet). It started as a claude.ai artifact and moved here the same day, because a plan in a chat is hard to find, revise or link.

## The spool cabinet, four revisions in two days

Revision A (2026-09-12) was a cabinet with acrylic doors and a heater box running at 75 C. The same evening it got a Fusion 360 script, a STEP file and a GLB, so the plan had an editable model and not just drawings.

- **B** lowered the dryer to 65 C. The shelf holds PLA, PETG and TPU, and PLA should not be stored much above 55 C.
- **C** fixed the shelves with pocket screws and 1x3 aprons. Clip shelves on a loaded 46 in span would creep.
- **D** (2026-09-13) rebuilt the case around IKEA OXBERG tempered-glass doors, dropped the heater box, and added a shopping list where every price was checked against a listing. Drying became a job for a bought dehydrator.

A two-bay version holding 112 spools lasted about an hour before it was trimmed to one BILLY-width bay: two OXBERG doors, 56 spools standing edge-on at a 4 in pitch. The earlier elevations had drawn the spools as circles seen from the front, which was wrong for spools sitting along the rails; the owner caught it, and the pitch and the drawings were corrected together.

The first price estimates were off in both directions: too high for plywood and studs, too low for acrylic and poplar. From then on a price was a listing read that day with the store named, or it stayed out of the total.

## The garage shop, a whole room

The [garage shop](/projects/garage-shop) (2026-09-18) was a bigger problem: a room with a parked truck, a wall cabinet, rolling tools, a dust cart and a subpanel. Revision A was drawn on an assumed garage. Revision B (2026-09-19) was drawn on the measured one, with a landing across the back wall, a house door that sat somewhere other than where the photos suggested, and no table saw on hand.

A room with this many moving parts could not be checked by eye on a flat drawing.

## shopcad: one module, every output

So on 2026-09-18 the CAD became a framework. Each build is one Python module. Its `DEFAULTS` hold every number a person would change, in inches. A `build` function places boxes and cylinders by those numbers, and a `checks` function returns pass or fail with the figures in the message.

From that one module come:

- a three.js viewer with states (doors open, bench flipped);
- a STEP file for the scene and one per build;
- PNG renders for the sheets, shot by headless Chrome with software WebGL;
- a Fusion 360 script whose user parameters are the same numbers;
- the checks, which stop the export when a number stops working.

The spool cabinet was rebuilt on it the same day, with ten checks. The garage reached 24 checks at revision B and 40 later. The rule that came out of it: when the model disagrees with a sheet, the model is usually right, because it had to close the geometry. A cabinet on a French cleat stands 1.5 in off the wall whether or not the sheet says so.

## Writing it down as skills

The workflow went into a Claude Code plugin called `workshop`, in a personal skill marketplace kept in the homelab repo. It has two skills. `build-plan` owns the design dialogue, the engineering checks, the drawing set, the prices and the delivery. `shopcad-model` owns the plan module and its outputs. Version 1.1.0 folded in what the garage and the rock screen had taught, so the next plan does not relearn it.

## The rock screen, a small jig

The [rock screen](/projects/rock-screen) (2026-09-19) cleans river rock from under a pool into 50 lb burlap sacks. It is a 24 x 48 in tilt tray on a 2x4 stand, pivoted 9 in behind the discharge end so it tips 35 degrees with about 23 lb at the handle when loaded, and pours through a plywood funnel into a sack standing on a hand truck.

Its drawings are generated by a script from geometry constants rather than drawn by hand. The sack size drives the spout, and the 50 lb sack forced an 11 x 11 in spout down to 9 x 9. One constant changed, and the funnel, the cut list and the copy followed. The fill rule became a sheet line too: rock runs about 100 lb a cubic foot, so a sack is three shovels, 45 lb, and about 60 sacks make a yard.

## Then the backyard

The next plan was a deck and a patio drawn from drone photos, and it pushed the framework outdoors and into photo-style rendering. That story is in [a backyard, drawn from drone photos](/writing/backyard-from-drone-photos); the plan is at [/projects/backyard](/projects/backyard).

## What carries forward

- **Every limit becomes a sheet line or a model check.** A swing radius, a working space, a sack weight or a shelf span is either printed on a sheet or enforced by the model, preferably both.
- **Prices come only from listings that were actually read.** The store is named, the date is recorded, and a price that could not be read is marked "not read" and left out of the total.
- **Pages are rendered and checked at desktop and narrow widths.** Each sheet is shot alone with the real fonts at 1100 and 720 px before publishing, because people read these on half a monitor.
- **The PDF is printed headless.** A print wrapper and headless Chrome make it, and the page count is compared with the previous PDF so narrow-layout rules do not quietly double it.
- **Plans live on the site, not in chat artifacts.** The [spool cabinet](/projects/spool-cabinet) was the first to move, and every plan since has started here.
